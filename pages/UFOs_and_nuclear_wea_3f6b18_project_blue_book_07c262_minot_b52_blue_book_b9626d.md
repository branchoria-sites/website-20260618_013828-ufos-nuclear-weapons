## Introduction

Among the many UFO reports associated with military nuclear facilities, the October 1968 Minot Air Force Base case stands out because of the unusually large official record it generated. Unlike many nuclear-related UFO stories that rely heavily on later recollections, the Minot incident produced contemporaneous reports, witness statements, [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }})-related material, Strategic Air Command involvement and a substantial [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) file. For that reason, many researchers regard it as Blue Book's strongest nuclear-related case—not because it proves an extraordinary explanation, but because the documentary trail is unusually rich and allows competing interpretations to be examined in detail. The case sits at the intersection of a nuclear missile complex, a B-52 strategic bomber crew, ground observers and an official Air Force investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/8331502" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo+2Minot AFB UFO Case]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Investigation of UFO Events at Minot Air Force Base...7 Sept 2024 — Following the UFO events in the early morning on 24 Octob...</span></span></span>

## Why Minot Became the Stronger Archive Case

The Minot incident occurred during the early hours of 24 October 1968 around the nuclear infrastructure associated with Minot Air Force Base in North Dakota. The base hosted both Strategic Air Command bomber operations and Minuteman intercontinental ballistic missile facilities, making it one of the most sensitive military environments in the United States. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_Air_Force_Base" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Minot Air Force Base</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minot Air Force Base</span></span></span>

What makes Minot different from many other nuclear-site UFO reports is not simply the claim itself but the amount of surviving evidence. The file includes:

* Reports from Strategic Air Command personnel.
* Observations from a B-52 crew.
* Ground witness reports connected to missile-field activity.
* Air traffic control communications.
* Radar-related documentation and photographs.
* Project Blue Book correspondence and investigative paperwork. ## What Happened Near Minot Air Force Base

The central event involved a B-52H bomber returning to Minot during a training mission. Air traffic controllers and military personnel reported an [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) aerial object in the vicinity. The bomber crew subsequently observed an unusual target visually and on airborne radar. ## How Blue Book Handled the Investigation

The Minot case entered the normal Project Blue Book process through official Air Force reporting channels. Investigating officers gathered witness information, submitted reporting forms and responded to requests for supplementary material from Blue Book headquarters at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. The surviving paperwork shows a far more detailed exchange than is available for many other late-period Blue Book cases. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/8331502" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Investigation of UFO Events at Minot Air Force Base...7 Sept 2024 — Following the UFO events in the early morning on 24 Octob...</span></span></span>

Blue Book ultimately concluded that the sighting was most likely attributable to atmospheric and radar phenomena rather than an unknown craft. The official file characterised the event as an "identified" case and associated portions of the observations with plasma or ball-lightning-type effects, together with radar anomalies and possible astronomical influences. The final paperwork classified the case under an "Identified (Other)" category rather than leaving it [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://minotb52ufo.com/investigation/section-6.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minotb52ufo.com">[Minot AFB UFO Case]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minotb52ufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">section 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minot AFB UFO CaseInvestigation: Section 6. This conclusion is important because it demonstrates how Blue Book approached complex cases. The project did not deny that witnesses saw something. Instead, investigators attempted to break the event into components and assign conventional explanations to each component. In the Minot file, the official solution was effectively a composite explanation rather than a single definitive identification. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1968-10-7170577-MinotAFB-NorthDakota.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikimedia.org.</span></span></span>

That distinction helps explain why the case remains controversial. Critics of Blue Book argue that a collection of partial explanations does not fully account for all reported observations. Defenders of the investigation argue that complex events often involve multiple ordinary causes perceived as a single incident.
