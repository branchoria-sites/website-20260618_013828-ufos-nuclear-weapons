## Introduction

The Minot Air Force Base case of 24 October 1968 remains one of the strongest tests of how [radar evidence]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}) should be evaluated in a UFO investigation. It is important not because it proves an extraordinary explanation, but because it preserves an unusually rich combination of records: witness reports from personnel associated with missile facilities, air-traffic-control communications, a B-52 crew account, radar-scope photographs, operational logs, and a formal Air Force investigation. ## Why Minot Is More Than a UFO Story

Unlike many UFO reports that depend primarily on recollections recorded years later, the Minot case generated documentation during and immediately after the event. The surviving archive contains [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) records, operational documents, witness interviews, maps, transcripts, and a sequence of radar-scope photographs taken aboard a B-52H Stratofortress. Researchers have identified more than one hundred pages of official documentation along with thirteen radar images preserved from the aircraft's radar display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://minotb52ufo.com/doc.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minotb52ufo.com">[Minot AFB UFO Case]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minotb52ufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Includes radar images, photographs, Project Blue Book records and interviews</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minot AFB UFO CaseDocumentation &#124; The Minot AFB UFO case &#124; 24 OCTOBER...Documents related to the UFO sighting at Minot Air Force Base on...</span></span></span>

That documentary density matters because it allows comparison between different evidence streams. A witness statement can be checked against a dispatcher log. A radar image can be compared with a communications transcript. Aircraft position estimates can be matched against reported timings. In most UFO cases, those opportunities do not exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://minotb52ufo.com/narrative/narrative-full.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minotb52ufo.com">[Minot AFB UFO Case]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minotb52ufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Includes hundreds of primary documents, including radar images,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minot AFB UFO CaseNARRATIVE &#124; The Minot AFB UFO case &#124; 24 OCTOBER 1968Scholarly examination of a UFO sighting at Minot Air Force Base on...</span></span></span>

As a result, Minot is frequently cited not because it is unquestionably solved or unsolved, but because it allows investigators to test competing explanations against an unusually extensive record.

## What the Minot Records Preserved

Several categories of evidence survived the original investigation:

* Base Operations Dispatcher logs recording reports from missile-field personnel.
* Radar Approach Control (RAPCON) communications with the returning B-52.
* Air Force witness questionnaires and interviews.
* B-52 radar-scope photographs documenting [radar returns]({{ 'radar-return/' | relative_url }}) during the encounter.
* Weather and atmospheric information used during the investigation.
* Project Blue Book analyses and conclusions. ### Correlating Radar and Communications

One reason the case attracts continuing attention is that the radar observations can be compared against recorded radio traffic. The communications transcript documents controller and crew discussions about the target's apparent position and movement. At one stage, the radar return reportedly appeared to close distance rapidly as the aircraft approached the base. Researchers have noted that communications difficulties occurred during a key phase of the encounter, creating another data point that can be compared against the radar timeline. Instead, investigators can examine the actual recorded displays. Researchers have used the photographs to estimate relative positions, compare sequential returns, and reconstruct portions of the encounter.
